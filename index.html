Two ways to build this

Both routes are workable and I am happy to deliver either. The difference is not only the monthly fee — it is how much of the result you control, and what happens when you want to change something later.

 Option A — configurator appOption B — built into your theme
How the preview works Options apps such as Easify swap one photo for another, so a merged bottle needs a finished image for every combination. Dedicated configurator apps (Zakeke, Kickflip) can layer images, which does work — but you supply a separate transparent layer for every colour of every zone. One master image and a zone map. Colour is applied live in the browser, so every combination exists without a single extra file.
Adding a colour A new layer rendered or photographed for that zone, uploaded and mapped inside the app. Type a name, pick the shade, save. Visible immediately.
Look and feel Styled within the app's settings panel. Anything outside it needs custom CSS on top of markup you do not control, and can break when the app updates. Built to your design exactly, in your theme, with no ceiling on what can change.
Page speed The app's script loads after your page and injects its own markup, which is felt most on mobile. Loads with the page. No third-party request.
If you stop paying The configurator disappears from the product page, and past orders lose the option details on screen. Nothing changes. The code is yours and stays in the theme.
When something breaks Theme updates and app updates can affect each other; fixes go through the app's support queue. One moving part, and I can fix it the same day.
Cost shape Lower to start, then a fee every month for as long as the store runs. One build cost, then nothing.
Best when You want it live quickly, you are still testing whether customers use it, and a fixed set of colours is enough.Fastest The configurator is the product, and you expect to keep adding colours and finishes.Recommended

What you manage yourself, either way

  • Colours — add, rename, reorder, adjust the shade, or switch one off temporarily.
  • Zones — which colours are offered for cap, ornament or base, since some finishes may suit only one.
  • Pricing — an add-on price per colour where premium stones cost more.
  • Badges — Limited Edition, New or Low Stock against any colour.
  • Defaults — the combination shown when the page first loads.
  • Wording — labels, help text and the section heading.
  • Visibility — turn the configurator on or off per product.
  • Artwork — swap the master image when a new bottle shape is added.
